Golfo Dulce is not, as a whole, a protected area. What surrounds it is a patchwork of designations — some marine, most terrestrial, each answering to a different agency and law — around a body of water that itself carries only a fisheries-management designation. Mistaking one designation for another leads directly to wrong assumptions about what is actually restricted where.

## Protected areas and marine management zones

The only designation covering the gulf's water column is the Área Marina de Pesca Responsable (AMPR) Golfo Dulce, created by INCOPESCA agreement A.J.D.I.P./191-2010 on 11 June 2010 under framework regulation A.J.D.I.P./138-2008 and Executive Decree 35502-MAG [@incopesca-2010-ajdip191]. It is routinely mistaken for a marine protected area but is a fisheries co-management instrument, not a SINAC conservation designation: it zones fishing and restricts gear under a Plan de Ordenamiento Pesquero drawn up jointly by INCOPESCA and the fishing organizations that petitioned for it [@incopesca-2010-ordenamiento]. Scholarly assessment is skeptical it delivers genuine co-management rather than reproducing centralized, top-down fisheries authority under a participatory label [@fargier-2014-ampr].

Around the water, SINAC units under the Área de Conservación Osa (ACOSA) cover different, non-overlapping pieces of shoreline and catchment. Piedras Blancas National Park occupies the entire northern shore and is the only park whose plan treats a strip of gulf water — about 1,200 ha — as protected area, with corals, mangroves and a needlefish spawning ground among its priority targets [@sinac-2015-piedrasblancas]. Reserva Forestal Golfo Dulce, 60,098 ha wrapping the western and southern catchment, is the main forested buffer between Corcovado and the shoreline, but it is a working reserve rather than a strict park — its main lever over private land is the payment-for-environmental-services program [@sinac-2018-reservaforestal] [@barrantes-madrigal-2024-acustica]. Refugio de Vida Silvestre Golfito sits within the same area, but no current SINAC plan specific to it could be located; the 2013 integrated ACOSA refuge plan covers other sites, not Golfito's [@sinac-2013-refugiosacosa]. Corcovado National Park, on the outer Osa Peninsula, shares ACOSA's structure, but its marine frontage faces the open Pacific, not the gulf [@sinac-2008-corcovado].

Immediately northwest of the gulf mouth lies the Térraba-Sierpe National Wetland, Costa Rica's largest mangrove system, a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance (site 782) since 11 December 1995 [@ramsar-1995-terraba]. Its area is reported at roughly 14,800 ha of mangrove, or 30,654 ha for the full Ramsar boundary [@acuna-piedra-2018-terraba] [@ramsar-1995-terraba]. It is not part of Golfo Dulce hydrologically or administratively, but its recognized uses — extraction for fuel and tannins, traditional fishing, clam and crab harvesting — establish the legal basis for the piangüero economy practiced in the gulf's own mangroves too [@ramsar-1995-terraba] [@silva-benavides-2015-anadara].

## Governance: three agencies, three mandates

MINAE, the ministry with overall environmental authority, delegates protected-area management to SINAC, which administers the terrestrial units and limited marine strips through ACOSA [@sinac-2008-corcovado] [@sinac-2018-reservaforestal]. INCOPESCA, an autonomous fisheries institute operating outside SINAC, manages the AMPR and fishing regulation across the water column instead [@incopesca-2010-ajdip191]. These are separate mandates sharing a coastline, not overlapping layers of one authority: SINAC's jurisdiction stops at park and reserve boundaries, and the AMPR does not reach the forest reserve shedding sediment into the water it regulates [@fargier-2014-ampr].

The land-tenure detail inside SINAC's own units admits how partial that control is. The Reserva Forestal Golfo Dulce plan breaks its 60,098 ha into Patrimonio Natural del Estado, private titled land, INDER holdings, untitled occupants, cadastral gaps and the Territorio Indígena Guaymí de Osa, and reports a management-effectiveness score of just 42% for 2016 [@sinac-2018-reservaforestal]. Tourism concessions run through a further layer: ICT tourism-aptitude declarations can exclude land outright, as they do for the Coto Colorado mangroves and estuary — declared non-tourist for their agricultural-livestock vocation, the same catchment carrying oil-palm and rice runoff into the gulf [@sinac-2013-refugiosacosa]. The contested Cocodrilo Bay marina proposal at Puerto Jiménez turned on whether municipal coastal-zoning instruments were ever submitted to SETENA for environmental review — a gap that produced a constitutional challenge before the Sala Constitucional [@sagot-2013-legal].

## Threats

Sedimentation is the threat every management document in the gulf treats as foundational. Sediment cores show more than 90% of what accumulates on the basin floor is land-derived [@hebbeln-2001-sedimentation], and thirty-three years of remote sensing confirm the mechanism: a 15 m riparian buffer of natural vegetation significantly reduces turbidity out to 800 m offshore, while pasture and gravel roads increase it [@brumberg-2026-turbidity]. Agriculture drives that sediment and the chemicals riding with it. African oil palm expanded across the catchment after bananas were withdrawn in 1984–85 [@clare-2005-palma], and the Coto Colorado sub-basin alone — 95% of the agricultural area draining to the gulf, roughly 10,000 ha of rice and 31,000 ha of oil palm — delivers pesticide residues found in every water sample tested, including the herbicide clomazone [@fournier-2019-agroquimicos]. SINAC's own Piedras Blancas plan documents oil-palm monoculture and grazing driving eutrophication of Laguna Machaca inside the park itself [@sinac-2015-piedrasblancas]. Fishers and tourism operators surveyed around the gulf independently name the same stressors: over 60% of both groups named sedimentation and agrochemicals as the reefs' principal threat [@villalobos-cubero-2023-arrecifes].

Fishing pressure is documented in detail for elasmobranchs. Golfo Dulce is a nursery for the critically endangered scalloped hammerhead — all 315 individuals sampled from artisanal catch were juveniles, concentrated in the June–August pupping season [@zanella-2016-martillo] — and observers on the legal bottom-longline fishery recorded 609 sharks across ten species in one year, over half hammerheads [@lopez-garro-2016-tiburones]. The piangua (mangrove cockle) fishery shows growth overfishing directly: at the more heavily harvested Golfito stand, mean shell length sits below the legal minimum [@silva-benavides-2015-anadara]. Coastal development carries its own risk: the contested Cocodrilo Bay marina alone would have stored 180,000 litres of fuel on floating docks, risking spills, wastewater failure and vessel noise for cetaceans that calve and nurse in the same waters [@gonzalez-2013-cocodrilo] [@herra-miranda-2015-marina]. Even Costa Rica's flagship land-use tool shows cracks: acoustic monitoring of payment-for-environmental-services farms in the reserve found its indices could not reliably discriminate forest condition, with the most fragmented sites sometimes scoring highest [@barrantes-madrigal-2024-acustica].

## Who is working there

Research capacity is concentrated in a small number of institutions. CIMAR (Universidad de Costa Rica) has produced most of the gulf's oceanographic and biodiversity baseline work, including the standard account of what threatens its unusually dense marine biodiversity [@morales-ramirez-2010-amenazas] [@morales-ramirez-2015-oceanografia] [@alvarado-2015-acosa]. IRET (Universidad Nacional) supplies the only quantified account of pesticide loads reaching the gulf [@fournier-2019-agroquimicos]. Fundación MarViva has published the only marine spatial planning methodology worked through specifically for Golfo Dulce, mapping conflicts between fisheries, shipping and critical habitat [@jimenez-2013-ordenamiento]. Fundación Neotrópica's executive director documents and helps lead the Frente por el Golfo Dulce, a coalition of Osa organizations allied with the national Frente Nacional por la Protección de los Humedales that contests development through advocacy, technical forums and litigation [@aguilar-2013-defensa].

Species-focused work is similarly concentrated. The Centro de Investigación de Cetáceos Costa Rica (CEIC), based in the gulf, produced the spatial analysis of dolphin and humpback critical habitat used against the Puerto Jiménez marina [@herra-miranda-2015-marina], and its researchers have since published the gulf's cetacean diversity baseline, population estimates for spotted and false killer whales, and habitat-use studies separating where humpback mothers rest from where adults forage [@oviedo-2015-cetaceos] [@oviedo-2023-stenella] [@pelayo-gonzalez-2022-habitat] [@sanchez-robledo-2020-pseudorca]. Asociación Conservacionista Misión Tiburón runs shark and ray monitoring aimed explicitly at INCOPESCA regulation, including the hammerhead nursery findings behind the case for a seasonal closure [@lopez-garro-2016-tiburones] [@zanella-2016-martillo]. A long-running in-water capture program has established the gulf as critical foraging habitat for hawksbill and green sea turtles — the eastern tropical Pacific's longest, most robust in-water hawksbill dataset, by its authors' own account [@chacon-chaverri-2015-carey] [@chacon-chaverri-2015b-verde].

On the community side, INCOPESCA's founding documents remain the clearest roster of organized stakeholders: the AMPR was petitioned into existence by fishing associations from Zancudo, Puerto Pilón/Pavones, Bahía Pavones, Puerto Jiménez and Puntarenitas de Golfito, with the national federation FECOPT [@incopesca-2010-ajdip191] [@incopesca-2010-ordenamiento]. A decade-spanning comparison of what these fishers and guides know locally, checked against independent survey data, found their knowledge genuinely useful for assessing flagship-species status — though narrower than a systematic survey [@bessesen-2021-lek].
