## What this is

This is a Phase 1 knowledge baseline for the Golfo Dulce, Costa Rica — a tropical
anoxic fjord-like basin, one of a small number in the world. It is not a survey. It is
an attempt to gather every piece of existing public knowledge about the gulf into one
place, sourced properly, and to say plainly where that knowledge runs out.

The site's real product is not the prose sections. It is the [gap register](/en/gaps):
a structured, evidence-backed account of what is not known, why, and what it would take
to close each gap. That register only has value if the claims feeding into it are
actually true — so most of the engineering effort here went into making sure they are.

## Methodology

### Every claim traces to a source, mechanically

The rule "cite everything" is easy to state and easy to let slide. So on this site it is
not a style guideline — it is a build gate. Every factual sentence in the prose sections
carries an inline citation in the form `[@source-id]`, resolved against a single
canonical bibliography (`bibliography/sources.json`, [browsable in full](/en/sources)).
A citation that does not resolve to a real entry fails the site's build outright; there
is no path for an unsourced or fabricated claim to reach a published page.

### Citations are checked against the real source, not just formatted correctly

A citation that looks right — a plausible author, year, and DOI — is not the same as a
citation that is right. Every source in the bibliography was independently resolved
against the [Crossref](https://www.crossref.org/) API at harvest time, and the title
Crossref returns for that DOI is compared against the title claimed for the entry. A DOI
that does not exist, or that belongs to a different paper, fails the harvest before it
ever reaches the site. This check has its own regression test — a fixture containing one
genuine source, one real DOI deliberately paired with the wrong title, and one invented
DOI — so the check itself cannot silently stop working without being noticed.

### Uncertainty is stated, not smoothed over

Where the published literature disagrees — the depth of the sill, the origin of the
basin, whether the deep water is "always" or "sometimes" anoxic — this site says so
explicitly rather than picking the more confident-sounding answer. Several early framings
of this project described the gulf as permanently anoxic; the completed literature
review corrected that. It is intermittently anoxic, renewed by episodic inflows from the
Pacific. That correction is recorded here, not quietly absorbed.

### The data pipeline is reproducible, not just a one-time download

Every dataset behind the bathymetry and occurrence pages was fetched by a script kept in
this project's repository, not downloaded once by hand and left undocumented. Each raw
file's checksum, licence, and retrieval date are recorded in a manifest, so a reader — or
a future version of this project — can verify that what is being served is what it
claims to be, and can re-run the fetch to reproduce it.

### Spanish is not a translation afterthought

The primary local and academic audience for this material is Spanish-speaking, so the
site is built bilingually from the ground up rather than English-first with Spanish
bolted on later. Where a Spanish translation of a page does not yet exist, the site says
so plainly and serves the English version rather than silently falling back.

## Corrections and collaboration

This site will be wrong in places — coarse literature reviews always are, and the gulf
itself is genuinely under-studied. If you know this water, or you are the author of a
source cited here and something has been misrepresented, please use the
[contact page](/en/contact) to flag it. Corrections from people who actually know the
gulf are the fastest route this project has to being trustworthy.
